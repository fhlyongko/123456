Scholarly Reading Support

How to Read Academic Articles Efficiently

Use this flexible reading sequence to identify a paper’s purpose, evidence, claims, and limitations. Read the research questions early, and revisit the introduction whenever context is needed. Article structures vary, so adapt the sequence to the study.

Step 1

Read the Abstract

The abstract condenses the entire study into a brief overview. Use it to decide whether the paper is relevant to your question.

Step 2

Read the Conclusion

The conclusion reveals what the authors ultimately argue. Knowing the destination makes the rest of the article easier to interpret.

Step 3

Read the Method

Check who participated, what data were collected, and how the data were analyzed. Methodological choices determine what claims can be made.

Step 4

Read the Results

Look at tables and figures first, then read the accompanying explanation. Distinguish the evidence from the authors’ interpretation.

Step 5

Read the Discussion

The discussion explains what the results mean in relation to prior research, theory, practice, and limitations.

Step 6

Return to the Introduction

After understanding the findings, return to the introduction to see why the study was necessary and how the authors justified the research gap.

Glossary

Key Academic Terms

AbstractA concise summary of the complete article.
Research QuestionThe question that a study seeks to answer.
Prior ResearchPrevious studies related to the current topic.
Literature ReviewA synthesis of previous research that establishes the need for the study.
MethodThe procedure used to collect and analyze data.
SampleThe participants or cases included in a study.
Quantitative ResearchResearch that analyzes numerical data and statistical patterns.
Qualitative ResearchResearch that examines experience, meaning, and interpretation in depth.
Mixed MethodsA design that integrates quantitative and qualitative data or analyses to address linked research questions.
Cognitive LoadThe demands placed on limited working-memory resources during a task.
Self-EfficacyA learner's belief in their ability to complete a task successfully.
ImplicationsWhat the findings mean for practice, theory, or future research.
LimitationsThe boundaries of what a study can claim.
Peer ReviewThe evaluation of a manuscript by experts in the field.
DOIA persistent identifier for a digital object, such as a journal article.

DOI Access

How to Find an Article Using a DOI

Add the DOI after https://doi.org/ in your browser address bar. For example, https://doi.org/10.30935/ojcmt/14195 resolves to the publisher’s record for Study 1. A DOI does not guarantee free access to the full text.
