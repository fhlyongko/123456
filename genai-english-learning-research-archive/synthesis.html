Cross-Study Synthesis

What the Seven Studies Say Together

This narrative synthesis brings together seven selected articles. It is not a systematic review or meta-analysis. The themes below distinguish reported experiences from teaching recommendations; recurring observations do not establish causal effects or independent replication.

Theme 1

Learners report both benefits and concerns.

Across the collection, students describe useful assistance alongside concerns about dependency and authorship. These reports concern experiences and perceptions, rather than measured rates of harm.

Study 1 · Study 5 · Study 6

Theme 2

The stage and purpose of AI use matter.

Studies of writing strategies distinguish planning, translation, revision, and feedback. Their findings support examining specific uses instead of treating all AI assistance as equivalent.

Study 3 · Study 5

Theme 3

AI-assisted writing can remain cognitively demanding.

Favorable course evaluations can coexist with moderate mental effort. Prompting and output evaluation are useful teaching priorities, but their separate effects require further investigation.

Study 4

Theme 4

Tool use does not establish readiness.

The readiness survey identified limited formal training despite tool use. This finding supports investigating instructional needs; it does not describe all university students.

Study 7

Theme 5

Instructional design warrants careful evaluation.

The classroom studies offer approaches to integrating AI into writing and speaking. Whether these approaches improve learning should be tested with suitable measures and comparison designs.

Future Research

Questions That Remain Open

The collection is largely based on learner perceptions and self-reported experiences. Critical readers should distinguish perceived usefulness from verified learning gains.

Learning Gains

Does AI-supported English learning lead to measurable improvement in proficiency, writing quality, or long-term language development?

Comparative Designs

How do AI-supported groups differ from non-AI groups when instruction, task design, and learner background are controlled?

Longitudinal Effects

Which benefits, habits, or risks remain after a semester or a full academic year of AI-integrated English learning?

Academic Integrity

How often do concerns about misconduct become actual misconduct, and what course policies reduce that risk?

Instructor Readiness

How are instructors preparing to design, teach, assess, and regulate AI-integrated English learning environments?
