Assessment / 04

Assessment follows the development of the manuscript.

The course emphasizes iterative scholarly writing: preparation before class, guided drafting during class, feedback-informed revision, and cumulative portfolio development across the semester.

Portfolio sequence

Four cumulative submissions.

Each submission makes one part of the research process visible. Together, they document the movement from initial problem formulation to evidence of revision and scholarly development.

  1. 01

    Research Question Brief

    A one-page brief that defines the topic, research gap, purpose, and research questions.

    Frame the study
  2. 02

    Literature Review Matrix

    A comparative matrix that synthesizes prior studies and identifies a defensible research gap.

    Synthesize evidence
  3. 03

    Manuscript Section Draft

    A developed draft of one major manuscript section, such as the abstract, introduction, methods, results, or discussion.

    Develop the argument
  4. 04

    Final Research Portfolio

    A curated portfolio that includes drafts, revision notes, ethical reflection, and presentation materials.

    Document revision

Evaluation criteria

What strong work demonstrates.

Evaluation considers the quality of both the written product and the scholarly decisions documented across the revision process.

Alignment

Research alignment

The purpose, questions, methods, data, analysis, interpretation, and contribution form a coherent scholarly argument.

Expression

Academic expression

The writing demonstrates clarity, accurate terminology, paragraph-level coherence, appropriate citation practices, and a cautious academic tone.

Revision

Revision process

The writer responds to feedback, explains revision decisions, and demonstrates measurable improvement across draft versions.

Integrity

Ethics and AI transparency

The work demonstrates responsible research conduct, careful data handling, plagiarism prevention, and transparent use of AI tools.
